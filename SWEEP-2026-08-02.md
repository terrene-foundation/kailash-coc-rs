## 5. Decision points (for direction — not self-decided)

### Decision point 1 — routing lane for #64 / #66 / SWEEP-A

All three touch **synced artifacts** (`commands/sync-from-template.md`, `bin/emit.mjs`, `commands/sweep.md`). Per `artifact-flow.md` § Distribution-Durability Invariants these are **Class-A non-durable** locally: a local edit is rebuilt away by the next `/sync-to-use`. The durable surface is a proposal.

**Recommend: originate one consolidated `/codify` Step-7b proposal covering all three**, rather than three separate ones.

- **Pro:** they share a root theme (the template's contract with a `coc-project` consumer) and one Gate-1 classification pass covers them; provenance stays in one manifest entry.
- **Con (real):** bundling means a Gate-1 reviewer who rejects one element may stall the other two; three separate proposals would fail independently. I judge the coupling worth it because #64 and #66 are the same underlying question — what a consumer may author locally and what survives a sync — and splitting them would fragment that argument across two reviews.

### Decision point 2 — disposition of #65

**Reconciled against ground truth: the fix has ALREADY landed in this template.** `sync-manifest.yaml:3339` declares `gitignore_reincludes:` carrying `"!.claude/bin/lib/"` and `"!.claude/hooks/lib/"`, and the manifest comment names this exact failure mode (the Python `lib/` block swallowing COC tooling, loom#676). A post-sync `sync-tier-aware.mjs::findSwallowedArtifacts` gate hard-fails the sync if any emitted artifact is still swallowed. Locally, `git check-ignore` confirms `.claude/bin/lib/` is tracked (9 files).

**Recommend: close #65 as already-delivered, with the receipt above, noting the reporting consumer needs to re-sync to pick up the reincludes.**

- **Pro:** the class is structurally closed upstream — both the negation entries and a hard-fail gate for future collisions. Leaving it open implies undone work and invites a duplicate fix.
- **Con (real):** I verified the _template's_ declaration, not the reporting consumer's post-sync state. If that consumer syncs and the library still does not land, the close was premature. This is why it is a recommendation and not an action — closing it asserts something about a repo I have not inspected and will not inspect from here.

### Decision point 3 — #43 (hono bump), INVEST-NOW vs defer

**Recommend INVEST-NOW.**

- **Pro:** a live HIGH advisory on the codex-mcp-guard HTTP layer; a dependency bump is ~0.25 cycle and the guard is a security surface. 43 days open is long for an advisory.
- **Con (real):** it is the one item in the queue with no consumer-reported impact, and a hono major bump could require guard-code changes beyond a version edit — the 0.25-cycle estimate assumes a clean minor/patch bump and would grow if the advisory requires a major.
