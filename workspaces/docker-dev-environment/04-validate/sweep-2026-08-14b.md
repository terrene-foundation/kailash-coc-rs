## 5. Decision points

### D1 — PR #155: the offer is relayed, but the PR is still open with no signal to its author

Entry 122 carries the offer's content plus six corrections, one blocking (it is a third
undisclosed claimant on `instrument-discipline.md`) and one substantive (as drafted it
condemns our own correct Gate C fix). The downstream author currently has no visibility
into any of that.

**(a) Comment the six corrections on #155, leave it open.** _Pro:_ the author learns why
their offer was corrected, and the blocking sequencing hazard is visible to them; costs
one comment. _Con:_ a public comment on a downstream-authored PR is a disclosure surface
and must be scrubbed; it also commits us to a review posture on future offers.
**(b) Leave it open silently until loom lands the relayed entry.** _Pro:_ zero disclosure
risk; the relay is what actually matters. _Con:_ the author gets no feedback, which is
how an inbox lane stops producing good offers — and this lane has produced our best
findings three days running.
**(c) Close it as relayed.** _Pro:_ clean board. _Con:_ **not recommended** — closing a
downstream offer whose content we adopted, before the adoption lands, reads as rejection
and destroys the lane.

**Recommendation: (a).** The lane's value is the reason to keep it healthy, and the
blocking correction is exactly the kind of thing an author needs to know. **Con of the
recommendation, stated plainly:** it is a public comment on a public repo and needs a
scrub pass before posting.

### D2 — the 320 unadjudicated files behind #150

The "62 SYNC-ONLY therefore safe" premise is refuted. Under Gate A's own query the
population is **28 eligible / 180 SYNC-ONLY skipped / 140 with no merge-base history**.
None has had a content review; the authorship argument that stood in for one does not
hold.

**(a) Content-review the 28 eligible files now, defer the 320.** _Pro:_ bounded, ~1 cycle,
covers everything Gate A can see. _Con:_ the 320 are exactly where a control-subtraction
would be invisible, which is the finding.
**(b) Content-review every file touching an authorization / signing / path-containment /
sanitization / fail-closed surface, regardless of bucket.** _Pro:_ targets the actual risk
class rather than the gate's visibility; ~1–2 cycles. _Con:_ requires a hand-maintained
notion of "security surface", which entry 124 itself flags as a weakness in #113's
proposed fix.
**(c) Hold everything pending loom re-issuing without the reversions.** _Pro:_ zero work,
and a re-issued sync makes the adjudication moot. _Con:_ we have no evidence loom will
re-issue, and no trigger that fires if it does not.

**Recommendation: (b), bounded to one cycle.** The risk is the class, not the bucket.
**Con:** it will not be exhaustive, and saying so is part of the disposition.

### D3 — the manifest has 104 STILL-OPEN entries and one consumer

Not a defect in any entry — a question about the surface. Entries have accumulated across
many cycles; the only consumer is loom Gate-1; we have observed no ingest of ours. Entry
123 proposes a fence that assumes Gate-1 processes entries.

**No decision is owed this cycle.** Surfaced because it is the largest thing on the board
and the one no local work can address. If a decision is wanted: the options are to keep
filing (current), to escalate the queue depth to loom as its own entry, or to stop filing
until ingest resumes — the last of which is **not recommended** and is named only so the
option set is honest.
